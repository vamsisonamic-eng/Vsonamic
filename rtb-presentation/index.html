The Fan-Out Broadcast

One page loads. Every buyer hears about it instantly.

Your phone or laptop opens a page with an empty ad space. That single viewing moment — an impression — is announced to a broadcast hub, which copies the message and pushes it down a private pipe to every buyer at the same time.

Watch the blue streams split: one shout in, five identical deliveries out. No buyer waits behind another; no one needs anyone's address.

Think of it like: an auctioneer with a megaphone. One announcement, and every bidder in the room hears it in the same instant.

The Algorithmic Race

Robot buyers think, price, and answer — on a countdown.

The buyers (called DSPs) aren't people. They're programs, each working for a different advertiser. Every one gets the same question — “what is this viewer worth to you?” — and a ticking 100 ms countdown to answer.

Inside each box: machine-learning models scoring the moment, budget checks, and a decision — bid a price, or stay silent. The green payloads are their answers firing back.

The Convergence Stream

Five answers, one funnel.

Bids come back from everywhere at once — so before anyone can be judged, they're pulled into a single stream. The aggregator ring you're looking at is that funnel: every green payload threads through it into one orderly queue.

This matters more than it looks: the judge should never chase scattered answers. The answers come to the judge.

Think of it like: auction paddles all pointing to one clerk, who lines the offers up in a neat stack for the auctioneer.
Under the hood direct exchangebids queue
4
The Time-Window Arena

The window is open. Then it isn't.

Bids fall into a glowing arena that only accepts entries while its time window is open. When the shutter drops, whatever is inside gets compared, and the highest offer above the reserve price wins.

See the bids bouncing off the closed shutter? Those arrived late. They aren't rejected for being low — they're never counted at all. In this world, speed isn't an advantage; it's the ticket to enter.

5
King Determination & Caching

Crown the winner, stash it in the vault, paint the screen.

The champion bid is written into a lightning-fast memory vault — a sticky note the whole system can read in a microsecond, filed under that exact impression's ID.

The ad server on your side of the internet reads the vault, fetches the winning advertisement, and paints it into the empty rectangle. The page finishes loading; you never saw any of it happen. And the loop is already firing again for the next ad space.

Think of it like: the auctioneer bangs the gavel, the clerk records the sale, and the artwork is on the wall before the audience looks up.

The big picture

A laptop-sized prototype of a planet-sized machine.

This entire pipeline can be built small — a message broker as the megaphone, a few lightweight services as bidders and judge, an in-memory store as the vault. The skeleton — announce → fan out → race → converge → judge → crown → serve — is exactly what runs at global scale.

By 2026, auctions like this move roughly 90% of all display advertising money, speaking a shared industry language called OpenRTB (currently v2.6).
