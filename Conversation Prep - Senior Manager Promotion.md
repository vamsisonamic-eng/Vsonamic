# Conversation Prep: Senior Manager Promotion & Compensation Alignment

**With:** Your reporting Director / dentsu India leadership (the promotion decision-maker), HR likely present
**About:** Formalizing the Senior Manager title + aligning compensation (₹13.10L → ₹30L) to scope already performed
**Your Goal:** Walk out with either (a) a committed "yes" to Senior Manager at ₹30L, or (b) a clear, dated path and mechanism to get there — not an open-ended "we'll see."
**Their Likely Goal:** Acknowledge your value, but protect the comp budget and the standard promotion cycle. They want to avoid setting a precedent and to keep you motivated without a large immediate payout.

---

## Before You Start

**Mindset:** This is not a confrontation, and it is not you asking for a favor. The client already promoted you — you are asking dentsu to *catch up to a decision the market and the client have already made.* You are presenting a business case for de-risking a $275M account, not pleading for a raise.

**Timing:** You have the July 5 meeting set — good. Make sure it's a real working block (30+ min), not squeezed before another call. Walk in having sent the deck (or a 1-page summary) 24–48h ahead so they've seen the numbers and aren't reading live. Never negotiate the number over chat afterward — keep it in the room or on a call.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't lead with the rupee number. Lead with scope and risk; let the number land *after* they've agreed the scope is Senior Manager.
- Don't frame it emotionally ("I've worked so hard," "it's unfair"). The deck already removed that language — keep your voice matching it: calm, evidence-led.
- Don't accept a vague "let's revisit later" without a date and a mechanism attached.

---

## The Opening

**Option 1 (Direct):**
"Thank you for the time. I want to be clear up front about why we're here: the client has already moved me to Senior Manager on Meijer, effective July 1. My ask today is for dentsu to align the title and compensation to the scope I'm already accountable for — and to agree the path to close that today."

**Option 2 (Softer — context first):**
"I appreciate you making the time. My intention today isn't to walk you through volume of work — it's to show how the *scope of responsibility* on Meijer has evolved, and to align on what that means for my role and compensation. I think the case is straightforward, and I'd like a clear decision or path by the end of this."

**Option 3 (Question-led):**
"Before I take you through this — can I ask: when you look at who carries the day-to-day risk on the $275M Meijer book, who do you see as the owner? … Right. That's exactly what I want to formalize today."

---

## Key Points to Make

1. **The client moved first.** — "Raymond Hall and New Stream Media recommended this promotion and elevated my billing to Senior Manager from July 1. This isn't a self-assessment — it's client-initiated and already documented."

2. **Scope, not tenure.** — "I'm not asking based on time served. I've been operating at Senior Manager scope for 18+ months — sole pacing owner of a $275M, five-platform account with no backup, holding >98% delivery-in-full."

3. **This is a revenue-protection decision.** — "Under-delivery is the number-one driver of advertiser churn in retail media. Formalizing this role is continuity insurance on a $275M book — it's cheaper than the risk of leaving it improvised."

4. **I'm a system, not just an operator.** — "The pacing and FMEA framework I built is now a shared standard — I've upskilled an AD, three Managers and an SM. Promoting me institutionalizes capability the agency can reuse; it doesn't create a single point of dependency, it removes one."

5. **The ask is structured and reasonable.** — "₹28L base sits *at* market for Senior Manager RMN scope — not above it. The additional ₹2L is a retention and proven-results bonus tied to 12-month Meijer continuity. Total ₹30L. The structure is flexible; the floor is the scope being recognized."

---

## Likely Pushback & How to Respond

### If they say: "Compensation cycles are fixed — we can't do this off-cycle."
**Respond with:** "I understand cycle governance, and I'm not asking you to break it. My request isn't based on timing — it's based on a role expansion the client has already formalized. If the cycle constrains *when* the money moves, then what I'd like to agree *today* is the mechanism and the date: an interim adjustment now, or a committed effective date with the number locked. I'd rather align on the path than defer the recognition indefinitely."

### If they say: "This was a team effort — it's not all you."
**Respond with:** "Completely agree, and I'd never position it as individual heroics. My contribution was building the *operating structure* that made the team repeatable — pacing governance, delivery controls, the FMEA SOPs, the trainings. The proof is that I upskilled an AD and three Managers on it. I'm asking to be recognized as the person who built the system, not just one who ran a task."

### If they say: "The budget is tight right now."
**Respond with:** "Understood — and my ask isn't benchmarked against inflation or a market bump. It's benchmarked against expanded commercial responsibility I'm already carrying and the revenue exposure I'm already protecting. If the full number is hard this quarter, I'd rather discuss a phased structure — an immediate base step, a committed timeline, and a milestone review — than have the recognition pushed out with no anchor."

### If they say: "Why you and not others at your level?"
**Respond with:** "Because my impact compounds. I'm not just increasing my own output — I'm improving the system that lets the agency run a larger, lower-risk account with the same headcount. That's the difference between an operator and someone ready to lead one."

### If they say: "Senior Manager is a big jump from where you are."
**Respond with:** "On paper, yes. In practice it's the title catching up to the work — the client already made the jump on July 1. I'm asking dentsu to recognize the band I'm already operating in, with 18 months of delivery and client endorsement behind it."

### If they get emotional or defensive (feel cornered):
**Respond with:** "I want to be clear — I'm raising this *because* I'm invested in staying and growing here, not as an ultimatum. I'd like us to solve this together. What would you need to see to feel confident moving this forward?"

### If they shut down or won't commit:
**Respond with:** "I don't need a final answer in this room. What I'd like is two things: do you agree the scope I've demonstrated supports this progression — and if yes, what specific path, timeline, and milestones move it to closure? Could we put a date on the calendar to confirm?"

---

## Your Non-Negotiables

*Fill in your real floor before you walk in — this is the one thing only you can decide.*

- **Must have (minimum acceptable):** Verbal agreement that your scope = Senior Manager, **plus** a concrete mechanism and date (not "we'll see"). E.g., "Title confirmed effective July, base reviewed at [date]."
- **Ideal outcome:** Senior Manager confirmed + ₹30L (₹28L base + ₹2L retention) agreed now or with a locked near-term effective date.
- **Walk-away point:** If they offer *neither* the title *nor* any compensation mechanism/timeline — only an open-ended "later" — that's your signal to say: "I understand. I'll need to think about what that means for me, given the client has already moved." Then stop. Don't threaten; let the silence carry the weight.

> **Decision you still owe yourself:** what is the *base number* below which you'd rather take the phased deal (interim bump + 6-month review) than accept a flat "no for now"? Pick it before the meeting. If you tell me your floor, I'll tighten the script's fallback around it.

---

## How to Close

- **If it goes well:** "Thank you — this means a lot. So to confirm what we've agreed: [Senior Manager title, effective X; base of ₹__ , reviewed/effective by ___]. I'll follow up with a short note capturing this so we're aligned. Appreciate you backing this."

- **If it's unresolved:** "I appreciate you hearing the case. I'd like to revisit this with a decision by [specific date]. Can we both commit to that, and can I send you the one-pager so HR has the scope and client endorsement on file?"

- **If it goes badly:** "I hear you, and I respect the constraints. I'm going to be candid that the gap between the scope I'm carrying and the recognition is something I'll need to weigh. I value this team and the Meijer work — I'd genuinely like to find a path, so I hope we can keep this open."
